before you ask

The questions founders actually ask.

How long does an MVP take?

It depends on scope, and anyone who quotes you a number before understanding your idea is guessing. What I can tell you is how I work: we agree on the smallest version that is genuinely useful to your first users, and I give you a realistic range for that before we start — not an optimistic one I’d have to walk back later. If the scope you want can’t fit the timeline you need, I’ll say so on the first call.

Do I own the code?

Yes — all of it. The repository, the accounts, the App Store and Google Play listings, the keys. Everything is in your name and handed over as we go, not at the end. You are never in a position where you need me in order to keep your own product running.

How do we communicate during a build?

You get regular written updates and working builds you can actually open on your phone, so progress is something you can see rather than something you’re told about. We agree on a check-in rhythm at the start that fits how involved you want to be. No status theatre, and no month-long silences.

Can you take over a project another developer started?

Yes — this is a big part of what I do. I start with an audit of what exists: what works, what’s broken, what’s worth keeping and what will cost you more to patch than to rebuild. You get that assessment honestly, even when the answer is that less of it is salvageable than you hoped.

What’s the difference between the Udemy courses and 1:1 training?

The courses are self-serve and teach general skills on example projects — great value, but you’re on your own when your specific thing breaks. The 1:1 program is built around your project: we build the app you actually want to launch, I explain every decision as we make it, and you finish able to vibe code new features, ship, change and maintain it yourself. It’s the difference between watching someone build and building yours with help.

I have an idea but no technical background — can you still help?

Yes, and it’s most of who I work with. You don’t need to know what React Native is, and you won’t be asked to make technical decisions you have no basis for making. My job is to translate: to tell you what a choice costs, what it buys you, and what I’d do in your position — and then to build it.
